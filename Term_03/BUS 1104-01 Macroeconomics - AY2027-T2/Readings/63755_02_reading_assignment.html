Unit 2 Reading Assignment

As you go through the readings and watch the videos, consider the following:
  1. What are the key tools for measuring inflation and addressing biases? 
  2. How do the various price indices impact economic decision-making? 
  3. What are the pros and cons of low to moderate inflation rates? 
  4. What is the role of indexing in mitigating inflation's negative effects?

Read

  1. Shapiro, D., MacDonald, D., Greenlaw, S. A., Dodge, E., Gamez, C., Jauregui, Andres., Keenan, D., Moledina, A., Richardson, C., & Sonenshine, R.  (2023). Principles of macroeconomics (3rd ed.). OpenStax. https://openstax.org/details/books/principles-macroeconomics-3e licensed under CC 2.0.
      • Read Chapter 8: Unemployment 
        • Section 8.1: How Economists Define and Compute Unemployment Rate 
          • Section 8.1 introduces the fundamental concepts of labor market analysis. This section provides a comprehensive understanding of how to calculate the labor force participation rate and unemployment rate. It also introduces the concept of hidden unemployment, offering insights into the nuances of labor force categorization and the challenges of measuring unemployment accurately. 
        • Section 8.2: Patterns of Unemployment 
          • Section 8.2 discusses the causes and effects of unemployment, including cyclical and structural factors and their social and economic consequences, such as income inequality and broader societal impacts. 
        • Section 8.3: What Causes Changes in Unemployment over the Short Run 
          • Section 8.3 explores cyclical unemployment, attributing variations to macroeconomic shifts, while introducing the concept of sticky wages and theories explaining their resistance to downward adjustments, impacting unemployment dynamics during economic cycles.  
        • Section 8.4: What Causes Changes in Unemployment over the Long Run
          • Section 8.4 explores the potential policy responses to unemployment, providing insights into how governments can address cyclical and structural unemployment. It explains how these responses involve fiscal and monetary policy measures, workforce development programs, and labor market reforms aimed at reducing the natural rate of unemployment.
      • Read Chapter 9: Inflation  
        • Section 9.1: Tracking Inflation 
        • Section 9.2: How to Measure Changes in the Cost of Living 
          • Sections 9.1 and 9.2 explore the calculation of inflation rates and the importance of the Consumer Price Index (CPI) in assessing the cost of living. These sections also introduce core inflation, which focuses on underlying price trends by excluding volatile elements. Additionally, they discuss various other price indices, like the Producer Price Index and the GDP deflator, serving distinct roles in economic analysis and policymaking. 
        • Section 9.3: How the U.S. and Other Countries Experience Inflation 
          • In Section 9.3, the discussion focuses on how various countries experienced fluctuating inflation rates, particularly in the 1980s and 1990s. It further mentions that in the early 2000s, the issue of high inflation generally subsided, though some regions, such as Zimbabwe, faced extreme hyperinflation. 
        • Section 9.4: The Confusion Over Inflation 
          • Section 9.4 explores the negative impacts of inflation, including unintended redistributions, blurred price signals, and planning challenges. The readings bring out that moderate inflation isn't a crisis, but debates arise over whether low inflation can enhance labor market flexibility.
        • Section 9.5: Indexing and Its Limitations
          • Section 9.5 explores the concept of hyperinflation, which is extreme and uncontrollable inflation typically accompanied by a complete breakdown of a country's economic and financial system. It delves into the causes, consequences, and historical examples of hyperinflation. 

Additional Readings 

  1. Girdzijauskas, S., Streimikiene, D., Griesiene, I., Mikalauskiene, A., & Kyriakopoulos, G. L. (2022). New approach to inflation phenomena to ensure sustainable economic growth. Sustainability 2022, 14, 518. https://doi.org/10.3390/su14010518 licensed under CC 4.0.
        • This article emphasizes the significance of controlling inflation for long-term economic growth. It covers macroeconomic indicators like unemployment and inflation, vital for sustainable development. 
        •  The article introduces a fresh analytical model for aggregate inflation, examines economic bubbles, and discusses their micro and macroeconomic consequences. Its primary contribution lies in the creation of a detailed inflation model, identifying key inflation drivers essential for controlled economic growth and sustainability. 

Optional Readings

  1. Gourinchas, P.-O. (2023, July 25). Global economy on track but not yet out of the Woods. IMF BLOG. https://www.imf.org/en/Blogs/Articles/2023/07/25/global-economy-on-track-but-not-yet-out-of-the-woods. 
        • This blog discusses the state of the global economy, noting its gradual recovery from the pandemic and other challenges. As you read this article, consider the global economic outlook. 
        • While it highlights improvements, such as reduced supply-chain disruptions and manageable inflation, it also mentions challenges like slowing growth and persistent core inflation. Understanding these real-world economic dynamics helps you apply theoretical concepts to practical situations and grasp the complexities of the global economy. 
  2. Ha, J., Kose, M. A., & Ohnsorge, F. L. (2019). Understanding inflation in emerging and developing economies (Paper No. 8761). World Bank Group. https://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/816031551363524517/pdf/WPS8761.pdf
        • This paper examines the substantial inflation rate decrease in emerging market and developing economies (EMDEs) and emphasizes the importance of stable monetary policies and strong economic foundations.
        • Understanding the factors and policies influencing inflation is crucial for making informed business decisions in these regions, where economic stability significantly impacts market conditions and opportunities.
  3. Press release. (2023, January 16). Economic slowdown likely to force workers to accept lower quality jobs. International Labour Organization. https://www.ilo.org/global/about-the-ilo/newsroom/news/WCMS_865256/lang--en/index.htm  
        • This report offers valuable insights into the global economic slowdown and its impact on workers. It highlights the risk of lower-quality jobs and growing income inequality. This real-world perspective bridges the gap between economic theory and practical challenges, enhancing your understanding of how economic conditions influence people and societies.
