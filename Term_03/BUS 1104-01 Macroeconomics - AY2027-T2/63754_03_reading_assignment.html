Unit 1 Reading Assignment

As you go through the readings and watch the videos, consider the following:
  1. What distinguishes nominal Gross Domestic Product (GDP) from real GDP, and why is adjusting for inflation important?
  2. How do economic fluctuations affect real GDP tracking?
  3. Why is GDP per capita used to compare economic well-being across nations, and what are its limitations?
  4. What factors drive economic growth?
  5. How does education influence innovation and development?

Read

  1. Shapiro, D., MacDonald, D., Greenlaw, S. A., Dodge, E., Gamez, C., Jauregui, Andres., Keenan, D., Moledina, A., Richardson, C., & Sonenshine, R. (2023). Principles of macroeconomics (3rd ed.). OpenStax. https://openstax.org/details/books/principles-macroeconomics-3e licensed under CC 2.0. 
    • View the online book.
      • Read Chapter 6: The Macroeconomic Perspective
        • Section 6.1: Measuring the Size of the Economy: Gross Domestic Product
          • Section 6.1 introduces the concept of GDP as a measure of an economy's size and covers its calculation from both the demand and supply sides, highlighting the significance of consumption, investment, government spending, and trade balance. It also addresses the avoidance of double counting in GDP calculations and mentions alternative economic measurements like Gross National Product (GNP), Net National Product (NNP), and Gross National Income (GNI). 
        • Section 6.2: Adjusting Nominal Values to Real Values
          • Section 6.2 discusses the importance of distinguishing between nominal and real GDP, introduces the GDP deflator as a measure of price changes, and explains how to calculate real GDP by adjusting for inflation using a base year. It also provides a method for approximating real GDP growth by subtracting the inflation rate from the nominal GDP growth rate.
        • Section 6.3: Tracking Real GDP over Time 
        • Section 6.4: Comparing GDP among Countries
          • Sections 6.3 and 6.4 introduce the concepts of recessions and depressions, highlighting their impact on real GDP. These sections also emphasize the importance of tracking GDP over time to understand the economic business cycle, as well as using GDP to compare the economic well-being of different nations, considering exchange rates and GDP per capita.  
        • Section 6.5: How Well GDP Measures the Well-Being of Society
          • Section 6.5 underscores that GDP falls short of capturing a nation's entire standard of living due to its inability to represent non-market activities, leisure, environmental quality, health, education, income inequality, and technological advancements. It explains that despite these limitations, GDP remains a useful, albeit rough, measure of economic well-being and its correlations with broader aspects of life. 
      • Read Chapter 7: Economic Growth 
        • Section 7.1: The Relatively Recent Arrival of Economic Growth
          • Section 7.1 discusses the onset of modern economic growth, primarily driven by the Industrial Revolution, during the last two centuries. It also underscores the crucial role of the rule of law and property rights in promoting economic growth and how variations in these factors affect different countries' economic well-being.
        • Section 7.2: Labor Productivity and Economic Growth 
          • Section 7.2 discusses the significance of labor productivity in fostering economic growth, examining factors like human capital, technological change, and economies of scale. It introduces the concept of an aggregate production function for analyzing growth sources and emphasizes the profound impact of sustained economic growth on a nation's standard of living, showcasing the substantial effects of varying growth rates.
        • Section 7.3: Components of Economic Growth
        • Section 7.4: Economic Convergence

          • Sections 7.3 and 7.4 discuss economic convergence, the process where low and middle-income countries grow faster than high-income ones, potentially reducing income disparities. Factors like investment in capital (physical and human), technological advances, and market-oriented economic systems play a role. Arguments for and against convergence are presented, with the process expected to be gradual, taking several decades for low-income countries to narrow the gap with high-income nations.
